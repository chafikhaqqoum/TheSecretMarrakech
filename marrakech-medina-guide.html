Marrakech Medina Guide: What to See, How to Explore and Local Tips

The Marrakech Medina is more than a historic centre. It is a living neighbourhood of homes, markets, mosques, workshops, cafés and monuments. The best way to experience it is on foot, with time to look around rather than rushing from one landmark to another.

By Chafik Haqqoum · Licensed Local Tour Guide · Updated 26 November 2026

Start at Jemaa el-Fna

The main square is a useful orientation point and a natural place to begin. From here, lanes lead towards the northern souks and the southern historic quarter.

Explore the northern Medina

This area includes the main souks, Rahba Kedima, the Mouassine neighbourhood and Ben Youssef Madrasa. It is a good introduction to the city’s markets and architecture.

Discover the southern Medina

The southern side leads towards Bahia Palace, the Mellah, El Badi Palace and the Saadian Tombs. Allow time to explore the streets between the monuments.

Look beyond the main route

Some of the most memorable moments happen away from the busiest lanes: a neighbourhood bakery, a small fondouk, a workshop or a quiet courtyard.

How much time should you allow?

A short walk gives you a first impression, but half a day or more is better if you want to enter monuments and explore different quarters. With two days, split the northern and southern Medina.

Practical tips

Wear comfortable shoes, carry water, keep your phone charged and save the address of your accommodation. If someone offers unsolicited directions or help, politely decline if you do not need it.
