Best Time to Visit Marrakech: Weather, Seasons and What to Expect

The best time to visit Marrakech depends on what you want from your trip. Spring and autumn are often the most comfortable seasons for walking around the medina, while summer and winter each have their own advantages and challenges.

By Chafik Haqqoum · Licensed Local Tour Guide · Updated 26 November 2026

Spring: March to May

Spring is a popular time to explore on foot. Days are generally pleasant, gardens are attractive and the city is lively. Book popular attractions and accommodation ahead during busy periods.

Summer: June to August

Summer can be very hot, especially in the middle of the day. Plan outdoor sightseeing for early morning or later afternoon, take breaks in the shade and drink water regularly.

Autumn: September to November

Autumn brings more comfortable conditions for exploring after the summer heat. It is a good season for walking tours, souks and day trips, though visitor numbers can rise.

Winter: December to February

Winter days can be mild and sunny, but evenings and nights are cooler. Bring layers, especially if you are planning an excursion into the Atlas Mountains.

How many days should you stay?

Two or three days is a useful starting point for the city itself. Add more time if you want a slower pace or a day trip to the mountains, coast or desert.

A local planning tip

Whatever the season, avoid trying to see every monument in one stretch. Group nearby places together and leave time for a meal, a tea stop and simply watching daily life.

So, when is the best time to visit Marrakech?

For most visitors who want to explore on foot, spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the most comfortable choices. Winter (December to February) can also be pleasant for daytime sightseeing, although evenings are colder. Summer (June to August) brings intense daytime heat, so plan outdoor visits for early morning or late afternoon and take a break during the hottest hours. The right season ultimately depends on your heat tolerance, budget and plans beyond the city.
