What to Eat in Marrakech: Moroccan Dishes and Street Food to Try

Food is an essential part of experiencing Marrakech. From slow-cooked dishes to quick snacks in the medina, the city offers plenty to discover. Start with a few classics, then follow your curiosity and try what locals are eating.

By Chafik Haqqoum · Licensed Local Tour Guide · Updated 26 November 2026

Tagine

Tagine takes its name from the earthenware pot in which it is cooked. You will find different combinations of meat, vegetables, olives, preserved lemon and spices.

Couscous

Often served for a family meal, Moroccan couscous is traditionally accompanied by vegetables and a rich broth. Friday is a particularly common day to find it.

Harira

This comforting soup is made with ingredients such as tomatoes, lentils, chickpeas, herbs and sometimes meat. It is especially associated with Ramadan but can be enjoyed at other times.

Pastilla

A special pie that combines delicate pastry with a sweet and savoury filling, often made with poultry and almonds. Recipes vary between kitchens.

Msemen and Moroccan bread

Try msemen, a layered pan-fried flatbread, with honey or other accompaniments. Bread is also central to everyday meals across Morocco.

Street food at Jemaa el-Fna

In the evening, food stalls bring a lively atmosphere to the square. Look for busy stalls with clear menus and food being prepared freshly. Check prices before ordering.

Mint tea and sweets

Moroccan mint tea is part of hospitality and social life. Pair it with chebakia, briouats or other local pastries.
